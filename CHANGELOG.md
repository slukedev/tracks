# What's New in Tracks

## Tracks 1.0

Motif is now Tracks, a new app with a new name and icon, made for playing and finding music
as much as keeping it.

Tracks opens to Play.

Tracks can now play your music on iPhone and Mac. Every song you play in Tracks is kept, even
with the app in the background, so your history no longer depends on having Tracks open.
Tracks keeps only what plays in Tracks: songs played in the Music app or other apps aren't
recorded any more. Your Last.fm history can still bring in what you scrobbled anywhere else.

### A New Look

- The crate leads Play: Tracks Radio in front, with this hour's mix, Discover and the rest of
  the day's mixes fanned either side, to flip through like records. The page glows with the
  colour of the one in front
- The back of the sleeve: tap the cover in Now Playing and it turns over to your history with
  the song: how many times you've heard it, when you first heard it and where, a year of it
  month by month, and when in the day it's yours
- Albums, playlists and mixes glow with the colour of their cover
- Now Playing's background is yours to choose in Settings: Full Cover, the cover edge to
  edge flowing on into its own colours with the type light or dark to suit, the artwork itself, Living Colour, the cover's colours flowing while the music plays, or Pulse, the
  cover's colours beating with the song, quick for lively songs and slow for calm ones.
  Choose how it changes between songs too: crossfade, slide or bloom
- Living Cover looks good on any cover: its colours are worked out in Oklab for each cover, so
  white and pale covers stay light without going flat grey, black ones show their flow, a flat
  colour gets a texture of its own, beiges and yellows never turn to mud, a framed cover flows
  from its picture, and the song's name and controls always read. It rests while you drag the
  player, and slows down when the device runs hot
- Full Cover keeps the top bar and the status bar readable over a cover's own lettering or a
  bright sky, with a soft wash only as strong as the cover needs, and its colours below the
  cover never turn to mud
- Top Songs, Top Artists and Top Albums travel through time: any day, week, month or year.
  Songs show when you listened, bar by bar, and a bar opens that day. Artists show who your
  listening went to. Albums show the record you went deepest into
- Find Artists is a feed of stories: an artist to meet, a few to try if you like one of
  yours, a song to start with, and a genre to go deeper into
- Find Songs is a pile to flip through: play, keep or pass, and play what you kept together
- Search leads with its best match, and opens on your recent searches and every mood
- Your Devices: your iPhone and Mac see each other, as Spotify Connect does. See what's
  playing on each, control it from the other, send the music there, or bring it here from
  where it's got to. Each says which speakers it's playing to, a HomePod or a group of rooms,
  and Play On lists your HomePods and AirPlay speakers beside your devices

### SharePlay

- Let the people with you add songs to Up Next from their own iPhones: invite them in
  Messages, or show a code on your iPhone or your car's screen for them to scan with the
  Camera
- In the car, SharePlay sits at the top of Up Next: the code, who's joined and the songs
  passengers added, with End SharePlay when you're done
- Passengers without Tracks join from an App Clip, with nothing to install: see what's
  playing, search Apple Music and add songs
- Passengers with Tracks can also add from their own library: Recently Played, Playlists and
  Recently Added
- Joining by code works with no network in the car, straight between iPhones, and over the
  internet when they can't reach each other. Every message is encrypted with a key only the
  code holds

### Tracks Radio

- Follows the time of day: your morning songs in the morning, calmer late at night
- Notices when you're driving, on iPhone, and plays for the road: songs you play in the car,
  steady energy and fewer surprises
- Knows which of your songs go together, from the songs you play in the same sitting. Let one
  play, play it again or make it a Favorite, and the songs you play with it come up from the
  very next pick, saying "Because you played" it; skip and they step back. It follows where
  you are and eases off over the next few songs, whether you have fifty songs or fifty
  thousand. The moods, genre stations, Handpicked and Autoplay do the same, from Apple Music
  or your own music
- Reads how you listen: a skip from someone who seldom skips counts for more, and a song let
  play counts for more from someone who skips freely
- A small library plays more new finds, so the first listens don't go round the same few
  songs, and one skip of a song you play all the time barely counts against it
- Skip as fast as you like: Tracks Radio, the moods, genre stations and Autoplay keep a song
  waiting behind the next while you skip, from the app, the lock screen, CarPlay or your
  headphones, and never skip off the end and stop. When the next song can't be reached yet,
  the one playing plays on and Tracks says so
- A genre's station stays in its genre however much you skip. Its new finds are only songs
  filed under it, and a small one that has played every song it has says it's reaching into
  the genres closest to it, never anything at random. It picks up where it was after Tracks
  closes, too, rather than running out into songs from anywhere
- Moods are truer to themselves: Energy, Workout and Party never play classical, ambient or
  sleep music, or a quiet acoustic take, and Sleep, Focus and Chill leave out metal, drill and
  club mixes, judged by each song's genre and by what its title, album and artist give away
- Create Station from the song playing, or start a mood or Handpicked from it, and the song
  carries on from where it is rather than starting again

### Play on the Mac

- Listen Now and Radio join the sidebar, with your library of Apple Music or your own music
  under them. Everything Play does on iPhone, the Mac does too
- The song playing floats on a glass bar at the foot of the window. Click the song for the
  full player
- Up Next and Your History rise from the bar (⌥⌘U, ⌥⌘Y). Reorder, remove, or double-click
  a song to play it now
- A Controls menu with Music's shortcuts: Space to play and pause, ⌘→ and ⌘← to skip, ⌘.
  to stop, ⇧⌘F for the full player, ⌥⌘R for Tracks Radio and ⌥⌘N to play something new.
  ⇧⌘M switches to the Mini Player and back, ⌥⌘M opens it alongside
- A Song menu for what's playing: Favorite, Add to Library, Go to Album or Artist, Your
  Stats (⌥⌘I), Share and Create Station. Go › Back and Forward (⌘[ and ⌘]), ⌘F to search,
  and View › Refresh (⌘R)
- Drag songs, albums and playlists anywhere: onto Up Next, or out to Messages, Notes or
  Finder. Every song has the same menu wherever it appears, Add to Playlist included
- Tiles lift under the pointer with Play and More, the charts and song lists work from the
  keyboard, and History plays a song on double-click or Return, with its details on ⌘I
- The Mini Player: the cover in a small window that stays in front, with the controls on it
  while the pointer is there
- The menu bar and the Dock show and control what Tracks is playing. Starting music in Tracks
  pauses Music
- Your Music plays FLAC and other files on the Mac: import songs or folders, or drop them in
  Tracks’ Music folder, which Add Music shows in Finder
- Stage and moving backgrounds hear the Apple Music song itself and move with every beat,
  not its preview. Tracks asks the first time, hears only Apple Music's own player, and
  records nothing. Settings › Play › Now Playing › Hear the Music turns it on or off

### Play

- Mood pages open on the covers of what you'd hear, under the bar, with when you play it
  ("Mostly Sunday mornings"), a glass Play, Shuffle and timer, and More Upbeat, Calmer and Less
  Vocal to steer it, even while it plays
- Genre pages read like Music's: the genre's name, how much you've played it and since when,
  then its station as a card of its covers, your essentials narrowed by kind, and the genres
  closest to it at the foot
- Moods, genres and Handpicked on Play are chips in two rows, as Control Center's modules are:
  the category's symbol on a disc of its colour, then its name. Search and a genre's page use
  the same chips, and on the Mac the rows page with arrows under the pointer
- Albums and playlists on iPhone open on their cover edge to edge from the top of the screen, with
  glass Play and Shuffle over it, its foot fading into the songs. The page ends as Music's
  does: release date, songs and length, label and copyright. On the Mac the header sits on the
  plain window

- Picks up where you left off: the song that was on when you closed Tracks waits, paused, for
  as long as you choose in Settings
- A new Play tab plays Apple Music inside Tracks: your library, live radio, stations you've
  listened to, albums, playlists and anything in Apple Music's catalog
- Tracks Radio, your own station: everything you love, weighted to what you play most, with
  new finds like it, picked one song at a time as it plays. Tune how adventurous it is, the
  genres it leans into, and whether it brings back old favorites, or turn it off in Settings
- Suggested Songs and Suggested Artists lead the tab: songs and artists you've never played,
  found by following the artists you love to the ones like them. Both shelves keep going
  for as long as you scroll them
- Find Songs and Find Artists go further: songs through a lens (your artists, artists like
  them, new releases, what's popular, or a mood), and artists by genre, each a tap from
  playing, adding to your library, or "Not Interested"
- Keep Exploring, straight after Apple Music's picks at the bottom of Play, keeps suggesting
  songs for as long as you scroll
- Offline Mode: without a connection, Play shows the Apple Music songs downloaded to your
  iPhone, the mixes they make, your most played and everything downloaded, all playing
  offline and kept in your history. Turn it on yourself in Settings › Play to save data. With
  Automatic Downloads on in Settings › Apps › Music, songs you add to your library from Tracks
  download too
- Find Your Mood: Feel Good, Energy, Workout, Focus, Chill, Love, Heartbreak, Party and
  Sleep. Go with the flow, your songs, new ones or both picked as it plays, or look through
  new songs and artists for the mood, your own songs that suit it, and Apple's playlists
- Tracks Radio and the moods pick each song as the one before starts, as a radio station
  does, so Up Next shows just the next song. Skip as much as you like: skipping an artist
  makes them rarer for the rest of the listen, and skipping new finds means fewer of them
- For You cards to swipe through: what you usually play at this time of day, and mixes for
  the rest of the day
- Your Artists: the artists you play most, with your play count, a tap from their page, a
  shuffle of your favorites by them or their station
- New from Your Artists, with everything from the last six months and what's coming soon,
  plus three rows of Apple Music's picks and its Top Charts
- Mixes made from your own listening: On Repeat, New Finds, All-Time Favorites, Deep Cuts,
  Radio Finds, Rediscover and a look back to this week a year ago. Save any of them to your
  library as a playlist
- Every mix says why it holds what it holds, and learns from you: songs you skip early
  twice, or ask to hear less of, drop out
- Shuffle spreads each artist out, so you don't hear the same one twice in a row
- Now Playing shows how many times you've heard the song, with a ring that fills as the song
  plays and turns to a check the moment it's kept
- Up Next to reorder, remove or jump to a song, Play Next and Play Last everywhere, Create
  Station from any song or artist, AirPlay and a sleep timer
- Your play counts appear beside songs on albums, playlists and in search
- Edit Play: reorder the tab's sections, or hide the ones you don't use
- Shake to Play: shake iPhone for a song you haven't heard that Tracks thinks you'll like, with
  Tracks Radio after it, from Apple Music or your own music
- Quick Switch, for people who use both Apple Music and their own music: Play's title switches
  between them, and what's playing carries on until you play something from the other. Play
  offers your downloads when you're offline, and Apple Music when your server isn't answering.
  A Focus filter picks one for each Focus, and Switch Music Source works in Shortcuts and on the
  Action button. Off by default, and only offered with both set up
- Your other devices: Play shows what Tracks on your Mac or iPad is playing, live, with its
  controls, and Play Here brings the song to your iPhone from where it's got to. The Mac's menu
  bar shows your iPhone's song the same way. Only your own devices, signed in to your iCloud,
  can connect
- Explicit Songs: on, Tracks plays the explicit version of a song that has both, even when
  your history, library or a playlist holds the clean edit, and a clean album opens as its
  explicit edition; off, it plays clean versions and skips explicit songs, stations included
- Song Transitions: none, or a crossfade of 1 to 12 seconds
- Siri: "Play music in Tracks" starts Tracks Radio, and "Play my mix", "Play something new",
  "Play On Repeat in Tracks" and moods like "Play Chill in Tracks" work too. Ask for any
  artist, song, album, playlist or station in Tracks, and Siri can learn to use Tracks when
  you just say "Play music"
- Ask Siri for anything Tracks can play, on iPhone, Mac and in the car: "Play Motion Sickness
  by Phoebe Bridgers in Tracks", "Play the album Blue", "Shuffle my Workout playlist",
  "Play Apple Music 1", "Play some jazz", "Play something to study to". Your library and your
  own music come before the catalog, and the songs you've played before the ones you
  haven't. When two of yours share a name, Siri asks which one. When nothing can play, Siri
  says why: no Apple Music access, no subscription, or no connection with none of your own
  music matching. Apple Music links play too
- Siri knows your playlists by name, Tracks' and Apple Music's, and the artists in your own
  music, before you ever ask for them. Your playlists and Tracks' mixes are in Spotlight
- "I like this song", "Add this to my library" and "Play more like this" work on the Mac too
- CarPlay: Listen Now with your moods, Radio, Library and Insights, with your week's top
  artists and songs a tap from playing
- Handpicked's picker searches everything at once: what you've played, your library and
  Apple Music, or your music and your servers. Suggestions like your picks join as you pick,
  beside Recently Played and Top Songs. Tap a cover to hear half a minute of the song: what's
  playing pauses for it and plays on after. Picks gather in a tray along the bottom, to take
  out or put in order, and the station opens with the first. On the Mac, Space plays the
  preview of the song you're on and Return picks it

### Your Music

- Choose where Play's music comes from: Apple Music, or Your Music, the music you own. Apple
  Music stays the default
- Play FLAC, ALAC, MP3, AAC, WAV and AIFF files from your iPhone: import songs or whole
  folders, open them in Tracks from anywhere, or drop them into On My iPhone › Tracks › Music
  in Files. Tags, covers and folder names are read for you
- Connect your own music server: Navidrome, Gonic, Airsonic, Ampache, LMS and any other that
  speaks the Subsonic API. Its whole library joins yours, streamed as you play it
- Trackside, Tracks' own music server that you run yourself: pair with it by scanning the code
  in its dashboard on iPhone, typing a code, approving a code Tracks shows, or signing in. Tracks
  checks that each address really is your server before it sends a key or password, and uses
  unencrypted addresses only on your own network. Its library syncs to Tracks and keeps
  up with changes as they happen, and Tracks finds it on your network and picks the fastest way
  to reach it at home and away
- With Trackside, songs play as they are, or converted to AAC when you're on cellular, set a
  quality limit, or the format won't play on Apple devices. Stars, ratings and plays go back to
  the server, and plays made offline are sent once it can be reached
- Moving from another server to Trackside: Tracks matches the songs you downloaded or put in
  playlists to the same songs on Trackside, and the downloaded files stay as they are
- Search asks your servers as you type, so songs newer than the last sync turn up too. With a
  music server that adds songs on request, search finds songs you don't own yet to play straight
  away, and Add to Your Music has the server fetch the file, which joins your music a few
  minutes later. What your server has shows at once; what it finds follows once you stop typing
- Picked for You, on each server: songs from artists you pick, play most or have, and artists
  like them. With a server that adds songs on request it works from an empty library and no
  listening history: pick a few artists, play what it finds, and add the ones you like, one at a
  time or all at once. Suggested songs such a server can find play and add the same way
- With a server, Your Music needs no Apple Music at all: Suggested Songs (artists new to you),
  Picked for You (artists you play), moods and Keep Exploring all come from your server, go on
  for as long as you scroll, and mix songs you have with new ones. Only Music I Have keeps them
  to what you have. Tracks asks your server for more a little at a time, so it isn't overwhelmed
- Picks follow your taste as it moves: recent listening counts most, the artists you picked
  give way as Tracks learns what you play, and songs you skip drop out
- Tracks Radio can start with songs already on your iPhone and download its new finds in the
  background: they wait in Up Next, play as soon as they're here, and can be swiped away
- From Your Downloads, and a Downloads page to look after them: storage, stats, most played,
  sorting, and Free Up Space for what you haven't played in months. Shuffle Downloads plays
  everything on your iPhone
- Playlists: make them, add songs from any song's or album's menu, reorder and trim them, and
  download them whole
- Smart playlists fill themselves: songs from your downloads or all your music, by artist,
  album, genre, year, plays, last played, date added or quality, in the order you like, as many
  as you like. They change as your music and your plays do
- Merge with Apple Music, off by default: playlists of the same name in Tracks and Apple Music
  become one. Songs added in Apple Music are found on your server and downloaded each time Tracks
  opens; songs added in Tracks are added in Apple Music
- Search finds albums and artists on your servers too, with ones a server finds elsewhere, and each has its page
- Suggested Songs, Picked for You and Keep Exploring show each song as it's ready, and stop
  asking your server for more once you've scrolled past
- Tracks Radio's Delete After Playing, off by default: new finds are downloaded, played, and
  removed. Keep any you liked from Up Next, or add it to a playlist
- Downloads use cellular unless you turn it off; then they wait, and start by themselves back
  on Wi-Fi
- Pause History, in Settings, keeps nothing you play until you resume, even across launches
- Download albums and songs from your servers, as the original files, to play with no
  connection. Downloads carry on in the background
- Discover on your servers: what's new, what's most played, songs like the artist you play
  most, albums picked at random, and Keep Exploring for as long as you scroll
- Tracks Radio, your mixes and the moods are made from your own music too, and "Waiting to Be
  Heard" turns up songs you own but have never played
- Hi-Res Lossless and Lossless are marked on albums and in Now Playing, with the bit depth
  and sample rate
- Albums play gaplessly, and the lock screen, Control Center, headphones, Siri and CarPlay
  all work with your own music. Every song is kept in your history, and your server hears
  about each play too, unless you'd rather it didn't
- Suggestions work with your own music too: every suggested song is checked against your
  files, your servers and Lidarr. Ones you have play; ones you don't show as Not Available.
  Choose Everything, Only Music I Have, or Off
- Lidarr: add artists and ask for albums from anywhere in Tracks, see what's downloading,
  wanted and coming soon, and download anything in your Lidarr collection through your
  server
- One Download for Apple Music and Your Music, off until you turn it on in Settings: albums,
  songs and artists offer Download on Apple Music, which adds to your library and opens Music
  to download, and Get on Your Music, from your server or Lidarr. It leads with the last one
  you chose, or always uses the one you pick, and shows what each already has: in your
  library, downloaded (iPhone), on your server, coming in, or Try Again

### Everywhere Else

- Search moves to the top of Summary and Play, and can search Apple Music, your library or
  your history
- Choose whether Tracks opens to Summary or Play in Settings. The tab it opens to sits on the
  far left
- Play on a song or album page plays it in Tracks, so the play is kept. Settings can send
  them to Apple Music instead, where they aren't kept
- Import your Last.fm history from Settings › Last.fm: it adds the songs you scrobbled from
  any app, skips the ones Tracks already has, and shows its progress in place. Nothing asks
  first
- Gone with recording other apps: recovering songs from Recently Played, Play Back and its
  control and widget queue, Save Current Radio Song, Treat Everything as Radio, and Background
  App Refresh
- Settings say less: shorter names, and a note under a setting only where it saves a mistake
- A welcome the first time Tracks opens on a device: Apple Music, Last.fm and Your Music in
  one place, none of them required. Installing again, or on a new device, shows Welcome Back
  and brings your history down from iCloud with how far it has got: "Restoring 12,400 plays
  from iCloud". A Last.fm account still in the Keychain is connected again without signing in
- Your settings survive updates, reinstalls and new devices. Play, Tracks Radio, Now Playing,
  Stage and device settings now sync through iCloud as well as History's, and a device that
  updates keeps its own choices rather than taking another device's. What depends on the
  device (downloads, streaming quality, your servers, where Play's music comes from) stays on it
- Summary's charts: tap a bar to read it and swipe sideways for the week or month before,
  and the page still scrolls from a chart. Charts compares your listening with the same
  point last week, month or year
- Summary on the Mac uses the room on a big display, and Top Albums always fills its rows
- Steadier and lighter: fixes for missed songs, settings sync, daylight saving in charts and
  streaks, playlist merging, downloads and CarPlay, and moving backgrounds that draw only
  as often as they need to
- Faster with years of history: pages open and scroll quicker, the first song starts
  sooner, and History's rows no longer shift as they load

## Motif 1.0

Motif remembers everything you listen to in Apple Music, including the songs Apple Music
doesn't count. This first release brings your listening history, in-depth statistics and
Last.fm scrobbling to iPhone and Mac.

### Listening History

- Every song you play in Apple Music is added to your history, whether you chose it or
  heard it on a radio station
- Radio songs are kept too, even though Apple Music doesn't count them as plays
- Songs that played while Motif wasn't running are recovered from Apple Music's Recently
  Played
- Skips are left out. A song is kept once it has played for 30 seconds, and you can change
  that in Settings
- Repair Artwork checks every cover in your history and fetches the ones that no longer load
- iCloud sync keeps your history up to date across all your devices, along with your
  settings and the songs you've removed, so a change made on one device holds on
  every device

### Summary

- Listening time for the week, month, year or all time, compared with the same point last
  period, with a bar for every day and a daily average
- Listening Rhythm shows when you listen, with a 24-hour listening clock, weekday and
  weekend averages, and your listening sessions
- Trends tracks your running total against last period, and how much of what you play is
  new to you
- Variety shows your mix of artists, your one-off songs and your deepest dive into a single
  artist
- Top Genres and Decades reveal the styles and eras you return to, with your recent
  releases, median release year and oldest song
- New Favorites, streaks and Records, from your biggest day and longest session to your
  earliest and latest listens
- Highlights sum up your listening in plain language
- Look back at earlier weeks, months and years: swipe the charts sideways, or use the arrows
  beside the period's name. Touch and hold a chart to read a single day

### Charts and Search

- Top songs, artists and albums for the week, month, year or all time, with how far each
  has moved since last period
- Artist, album and song pages show plays over time, rank, genre, release years, and when
  you first and last heard them
- Tap any album, anywhere it appears, for its own page and the songs you play from it
- Search finds anything you've played, including by genre

### Radio

- Heard on Radio collects the songs you hear on stations into a playlist in your Apple Music
  library
- Play Back plays your radio songs again through Apple Music so they count as plays. It only
  runs when you're there to hear it, and never plays silently
- Exclude any station you'd rather not have recorded

### Last.fm

- Connect your Last.fm account and Motif scrobbles every song it keeps, including radio
  songs and songs recovered from Recently Played
- Bring your Last.fm history into Motif. Right after you connect, Motif asks whether to
  import your scrobbles, dated when you played them; Sync Now in Last.fm settings adds what
  you've scrobbled since, from any app. Songs Motif already has are skipped, nothing is
  sent back to Last.fm, and an import that stops part way carries on where it left off
- History has a From Last.fm filter
- Your Last.fm session key is stored securely in the Keychain

### iPhone

- Summary, History, Charts and Search, each a tap away
- With Background App Refresh, Motif catches up while it's closed, recovering songs, adding
  covers and sending scrobbles. Settings shows when it last did
- Settings is a list of pages (Play, History, Radio, Apple Music, Last.fm and iCloud), each
  row saying where things stand, and each page opening with a line on how it's doing

### Mac

- A sidebar window with a dashboard Summary, a sortable history table with an inspector,
  top charts and a week-by-hour heat map
- A menu bar extra with the current song, its cover and playback controls, plus today's
  listening, your streak and your last few songs
- Show a symbol, the cover, the title, the artist or a format of your own in the menu bar
- A Settings window with General, History, Radio, Last.fm, iCloud and Menu Bar panes, each
  sized to fit and opening with a line on how it's doing

### Widgets, Controls and Shortcuts

- Listening shows your last seven days and your streak, with Lock Screen sizes on iPhone
- Last Played shows the last song Motif kept
- Today shows today's songs and the radio songs lined up for Play Back
- A Control Center control plays back today's radio songs
- Save Current Radio Song and Play Back Today work with Siri, Spotlight and Shortcuts

### Privacy

- Motif has no servers, no account and no analytics
- Your listening history stays on your devices and in your private iCloud database
- Last.fm is contacted only if you connect an account

### For Developers

- Launch with `-MotifDemoData YES` to explore every screen with sample listening history.
  It lives in a separate in-memory store and is never saved, synced or scrobbled
- Last.fm API credentials are read from a gitignored `Config/Secrets.xcconfig` and are never
  committed

Some features require an Apple Music subscription. Motif requires iOS 27 or macOS 27 or
later.
